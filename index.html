12-Month Roadmap

phases follow your response, not arbitrary dates
Phase 1 — Months 1–2: Stabilize

Goal: recover consistency after the 84→72 kg drop, establish food intake, logging, pain-free exercise selection, and medical follow-up. Training: 5 days with controlled volume, 1–3 RIR. Calories: ~2,400–2,500. Protein: 135–150g. Steps: ~2k/day, gradually upward. Expected: weight stabilizes or begins gaining ~0.1–0.2 kg/week; performance becomes less erratic. Move on: 3–4 consistent weeks, food adherence high, and weight trend understood.

Phase 2 — Months 3–6: Build

Goal: measurable muscle gain, especially legs, biceps, chest/back. Training: same core program with progressive overload. Calories: adjust only from 7-day weight averages. Protein: 135–150g. Steps: ~3–5k/day. Expected: gradual strength increases and visible fullness. Move on: stable gain without excessive waist increase and good recovery.

Phase 3 — Months 7–9: Build + refine

Goal: continue gaining while bringing up proportions. Training: keep major lifts stable; add volume only where recovery and progress justify it. Calories: maintain the smallest surplus that keeps progress moving. Protein: 135–150g. Expected: better arm/leg balance, stronger pressing and pulling. Move on: once you have enough data to decide between further gain and a short maintenance/mini-cut period.

Phase 4 — Months 10–12: Evaluate

Goal: consolidate the year's gains and decide the next direction. Training: maintain the proven program. Calories: maintenance or modest surplus based on waist, weight and performance. Expected: a noticeably more developed overall physique if adherence stays high. Move on: year-end review and next-cycle plan.

80–85 kg is a long-term direction, not a 12-month requirement. The dashboard prioritizes lean mass and visual improvement over a forced scale number.

Rules

the system that keeps the plan stable

Plateau rules

Weight not changing: first verify 2–3 weeks of consistent intake, then add ~100–150 kcal/day.
Waist rising too quickly: remove ~100–150 kcal/day and keep protein stable.
Strength stalls: check sleep, food, RIR, technique and exercise pain before adding more volume.
Muscle not growing: verify progressive overload and adequate weekly sets; only then add 1–2 sets/week.
Recovery poor: reduce sets before adding stimulants; consider a deload if fatigue persists.

Training rules

Most sets finish with 1–3 RIR.
Use failure selectively on safer isolation work.
Increase reps first, then load.
Do not force exercises that reproduce your back pain.
Keep the core program stable long enough to measure it.

Why your previous 3–4 years produced limited results

Confirmed from your history: the training period was inconsistent, there were repeated breaks, there was no formal workout log, nutrition was not tracked, protein intake was inconsistent, progression was informal, leg training was limited by back pain, and your lifts fell alongside a large recent bodyweight drop.

Reasonable inference: this combination likely reduced your total effective training stimulus and made progressive overload and recovery difficult to sustain. It is more useful to fix these controllable factors than to search for a special “body type” explanation.

Medical safety: chronic back pain and abnormal thyroid labs should be followed by qualified clinicians. This app is a training/nutrition tracker, not a diagnostic or medication tool.
